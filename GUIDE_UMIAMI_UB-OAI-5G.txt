 READ THIS FIRST — what runs today, and what does not
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 YOUR PORT IS DONE. INTEGRATION.md §6 phase 1 is complete and validated
 radio-free: the CLIP backend, the float32 embedding codec, the zero-shot
 classifier and the API port (semcom_core.py + phy_port.py + semcom.py).

 AND IT ONLY NEEDS TWO RADIOS. Semantic communication here is a point-to-
 point data transfer -- the base station sends one float32 embedding, one
 user receives and classifies it. That is the same byte-pipe FL uses
 (INTEGRATION.md §4: one TX, one RX). UB-OAI 5G's two X310s are enough.
 Nothing in this port needs a third radio, unlike the AirComp and
 multi-agent MARL ports.

 THE ONE THING TO UNDERSTAND BEFORE YOU BOOK TIME -- READ §3 OF
 INTEGRATION.md. Our PHY offers TWO REGIMES and they answer DIFFERENT
 questions:

   (1) RELIABLE -- what the radio backend does TODAY. CRC-16 + FEC +
       stop-and-wait ARQ deliver the embedding LOSSLESSLY. Accuracy is
       preserved, and the cost of a noisy channel shows up as
       RETRANSMISSIONS, DELAY and ENERGY -- not as degraded accuracy.

   (2) NOISY / SEMANTIC -- what reproduces the paper's Fig. 3. The
       embedding is sent WITHOUT a CRC gate, so residual bit errors pass
       through and corrupt the floats, and accuracy falls with SNR. Today
       this is the RADIO-FREE `--channel pyphy` path. On the radio it is
       phase 2 (single-shot `--max-attempts 1` with the CRC check disabled
       on this stream).

 SO: going on-air at UB today proves the TRANSPORT works end to end. It
 does NOT reproduce the paper's headline figure, because ARQ removes
 exactly the noise the paper is about. Plan your session accordingly --
 the Fig. 3 curve is produced radio-free in PART 4, and it is produced
 through our REAL modem, not a toy channel.

 TWO MORE THINGS THAT WILL OTHERWISE CONFUSE YOU:

   WITH --mock, ALL THREE CLIP MODELS SCORE THE SAME ACCURACY. The mock
   backend is a deterministic stand-in, so vit-b32 / b16 / l14 all report
   0.988 and only payload, delay and energy differ. The paper's accuracy
   ORDERING (Fig. 2, L/14 > B/16 > B/32) needs REAL weights -- that is
   phase 2, and it is one pip install, not a code change.

   FEC IS NOT OPTIONAL HERE. A 512-float embedding is wrecked by any
   surviving bit error. Uncoded QPSK at 6 dB has BER 0.034 and gives
   accuracy ~0; turbo at the same 6 dB is error-free and gives 0.988.
   Always carry --fec turbo on this payload.

 WHAT YOU CAN DO TODAY ON UB-OAI 5G:
     PART 4  reproduce the paper radio-free, through the real modem (verified)
     PART 5  prove your reservation and the two X310s are alive    (verified)
     PART 6  on-air embedding transfer, and the gap to on-air Fig. 3

 PART 4 — Reproduce the paper, no radio  (THIS WORKS TODAY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Do this first, every time. It needs no radio, so a hardware fault cannot
 confuse the result. This is also where the paper's Fig. 3 actually comes
 from -- see the regime note at the top.

 # once per session. The MOCK path needs only numpy + opencv, both of which
 # come from here. Real CLIP weights are a separate opt-in (step 4d).
 pip install -r requirements.txt

 # the platform's own smoke test
 ./run.sh selftest

 cd deploy/workspace/algorithms/semcom_umiami

 # 4a. END-TO-END SANITY: encode -> lossless channel -> classify
 python3 semcom.py demo --mock

 expect:
   [semcom] CLIP=ViT-B-32(mock,d=512)  spec=PayloadSpec(dtype='float32', shape=(512,))
            quant=f32  N=80 images / 10 classes  payload=2061 B
   [semcom] noise-free (channel-perfect) top-1 accuracy = 0.988

      SNR dB |      BER | accuracy | channel
         8.0 |   0.0000 |    0.988 | ideal/QPSK

 Read that: one image becomes a 512-float embedding, 2061 bytes on the wire.
 On a clean channel the classifier is right 98.8% of the time. That is your
 ceiling -- everything below is how much the channel takes away.

 # 4b. THE PAPER'S FIG. 3, through OUR REAL MODEM (turbo-coded QPSK).
 #     This is the noisy/semantic regime: no CRC gate, so bit errors reach
 #     the floats and accuracy degrades. Radio-free, but NOT a toy channel.
 PYTHONPATH=../../../../drivers/usrp/bindings python3 semcom.py demo --mock \
     --channel pyphy --scheme QPSK --fec turbo --snr-sweep 0,2,4,6,10

 expect (recorded in INTEGRATION.md §3 as the validated result):
    SNR dB |  BER   | accuracy
      0.0  | 0.208  |  0.000
      2.0  | 0.073  |  0.000
      4.0  | 0.001  |  0.700
      6.0  | 0.000  |  0.988     <- matches the paper's Fig. 3 shape

 The cliff between 2 and 6 dB IS the result. A 512-float embedding has no
 redundancy, so it is all-or-nothing: once FEC closes the last errors,
 accuracy jumps to the clean ceiling.

 # 4c. THE 3-MODEL TRADEOFF (the paper's Table I)
 python3 semcom.py demo --mock --model-sweep

 expect:
         model |  dim | payload B | t_tx ms |  energy | clean acc
       vit-b32 |  512 |      2061 |   0.039 |  0.0010 |     0.988
       vit-b16 |  512 |      2061 |   0.039 |  0.0013 |     0.988
       vit-l14 |  768 |      3085 |   0.058 |  0.0039 |     0.988

 NOTE the accuracy column is IDENTICAL for all three. That is the mock
 backend, not a bug -- see the top of this guide. Payload, delay and energy
 are the real axes here; accuracy only separates with real weights.

 # 4d. REAL CLIP WEIGHTS (phase 2, one pip install)
 pip install -r requirements.txt     # pulls open_clip_torch; torch is already here
 python3 semcom.py demo --model vit-l14      # dropping --mock uses real ViT-L/14

 load_clip() auto-detects open_clip. If it is missing or the host is offline
 it PRINTS A NOTICE AND FALLS BACK TO THE MOCK rather than failing -- so if
 your accuracies all look like 0.988 again, check for that notice before
 concluding anything about the models.

 PART 5 — Prove the UB radios are alive  (THIS WORKS TODAY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 This does NOT run your algorithm. It runs the platform's ARQ link over the
 two X310s, exactly as WINGS Lab runs it. Its only job is to prove that your
 reservation, the host, the cabling and both radios work -- so that when you
 move to PART 6 you are not debugging three things at once.

 START THE RECEIVER FIRST, ALWAYS. The source dials for the ACK, so a
 transmitter started first just burns its retry budget.

 # 0. find the radios
 uhd_find_devices

 # 1. SURVEY -- run on every radio that RECEIVES, receive-only, nothing TXes.
 #    Use the SAME --subdev/--ant that radio will receive on, or you measure a
 #    different port than the one you listen on.
 ./prepare.sh --device x310 --args addr=192.168.40.2 --subdev A:0 --ant RX2 \
              --band vert2450

 #    Results land in /workspace/experiments/searching/<timestamped>, and
 #    radio.sh / run.sh read them automatically. Add --dry-run first to see
 #    the plan and how long the sweep takes.

 # 2. SINK -- the X310 at .40.2. START THIS FIRST, leave it running.
 ./radio.sh rx --device x310 \
   --args addr=192.168.40.2 --rx-args addr=192.168.40.2 --tx-args addr=192.168.40.2 \
   --rx-subdev A:0 --rx-ant RX2   --rx-freq 2400e6 --rx-gain 20 \
   --tx-subdev A:0 --tx-ant TX/RX --tx-freq 2450e6 --tx-gain 25 \
   --role sink_arq --ack-transport rf \
   --det-mult 30 --sync-threshold 10 --scheme QPSK --bytes-length 1000

 # 3. SOURCE -- the X310 at .30.2, second terminal.
 ./radio.sh tx --device x310 \
   --args addr=192.168.30.2 --tx-args addr=192.168.30.2 --rx-args addr=192.168.30.2 \
   --tx-subdev A:0 --tx-ant TX/RX --tx-freq 2400e6 --tx-gain 25 \
   --rx-subdev A:0 --rx-ant RX2   --rx-freq 2450e6 --rx-gain 20 \
   --role source_arq --ack-transport rf --max-attempts 0 \
   --det-mult 30 --sync-threshold 10 --scheme QPSK --bytes-length 1000

 Data goes out on 2400 MHz; the link-layer ACK comes back on 2450 MHz. That
 is why each side declares BOTH an --rx-* and a --tx-* chain, and why both
 boxes had to be surveyed. --max-attempts 0 means retry until it succeeds.

 expect:
   [RX] chunk 1/5  [CRC OK, new]  (1/5 verified): "It is a period of civil war."
   [RX] all 5 chunks CRC-verified -- message complete, stopping.

 This link test is a GOOD predictor for your port, because semcom uses the
 same reliable-ARQ byte pipe. If PART 5 passes, PART 6 step 1 should too.

 If it fails:
   - No [ACQ] lines at all   -> nothing was detected. Same freq on both ends?
                                antenna on TX/RX to transmit? source running?
   - [ACQ] peak well above threshold but still failing -> the front end and
                                sync are fine; the problem is in the DATA.
                                MORE GAIN WILL NOT HELP.
   - [RX] burst N REJECTED   -> prints what it decoded and how wrong it was.
                                That line usually names the problem outright.

 PART 6 — On-air semantic communication
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 STEP 1 — EMBEDDING TRANSFER OVER THE RADIO.  >>> TWO X310s ARE ENOUGH. <<<
 INTEGRATION.md §6 phase 3. The BS encodes an image, the embedding crosses
 the air under reliable ARQ, the user classifies it. START THE RX FIRST.

 cd deploy/workspace/algorithms/semcom_umiami

 # USER / RX host -- receives embeddings and classifies them locally.
 # It builds the CLIP TEXT features itself, so there is no return link.
 python3 semcom.py rx --rx-args addr=192.168.40.2 --rx-subdev A:0 --num 8

 # BS / TX host -- encodes images, sends each embedding. Second terminal.
 python3 semcom.py tx --tx-args addr=192.168.30.2 --tx-subdev A:0 \
         --ack-host <RX_HOST_IP> --chunk 125

 SUCCESS CRITERION: a delivered embedding classifies the same as it did
 radio-free. Because ARQ is lossless, accuracy should MATCH PART 4a
 (~0.988). If it is lower, the problem is the codec or the label set, not
 the channel -- the channel either delivers the bytes or retransmits.

 WHAT TO MEASURE HERE: not accuracy -- retransmissions, delay and energy.
 That is the reliable regime's whole point, and it is the axis the paper's
 PPO optimiser trades against accuracy. --chunk 125 is the validated
 framing size; one image may span several chunks.

 >>> KNOWN GAP FOR X310 USERS: semcom.py exposes --rx-subdev / --tx-subdev
     (good -- the MARL scripts do not) but NOT --rx-ant / --tx-ant. The
     recipes were validated on a B210 -> N210 pair where the defaults are
     correct. Your X310s have antennas on the FIRST RF port only, and PART 5
     shows this link wants --rx-ant RX2 / --tx-ant TX/RX. CONFIRM the
     defaults select the port you are cabled to BEFORE the session, or ask
     WINGS Lab.

 >>> ALSO NOTE: INTEGRATION.md §4 describes this link at 915 MHz, because it
     was written against the B210/N210 rig. UB-OAI 5G's X310 pair runs on
     vert2450 (2400-2450 MHz), which is what PART 5 uses and what your
     reservation band covers.

 STEP 2 — ON-AIR FIG. 3 (the noisy regime).  >>> PHASE 2, NOT YET BUILT. <<<
 To degrade accuracy with SNR on the radio you need single-shot delivery
 with the CRC gate disabled on this stream: `--max-attempts 1` plus letting
 a failed-CRC burst through to the application instead of dropping it. The
 single-shot flag already exists; the "deliver it anyway" path does not.
 This is INTEGRATION.md §3 regime 2 and §6 phase 3's second half.

 Until it exists, PART 4b is the Fig. 3 result -- and it is measured through
 the real turbo-coded QPSK modem, not a simulated channel, so it is a
 perfectly defensible number to publish.

 STEP 3 — MULTI-USER (the paper's U=5).  Run several RX users; the BS sends
 each user's embedding in turn. Each extra SIMULTANEOUS user needs its own
 radio, so at UB you can only serialise them. True simultaneous OFDMA
 resource-block allocation is a phase-2 PHY feature (INTEGRATION.md §5),
 and it is what the PPO optimiser would actually control.
